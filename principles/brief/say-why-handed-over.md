# Say why it is handed over

**Claim:** a task file says why this work leaves the lead, with the lead doing it alone as the default.

- **Grade:** measured for the size and the sign of the gain depending on the task; convention for writing the reason into the task file.
- **Evidence:** [the review](../../evidence/multi-agent-teams/review.md) holds that over 260 configurations the change against one agent ran from +80.8% to -70.0%, set by the task (strand C, Kim 2025).
- Strand E adds two conventions: add agents only when needed (Anthropic 2024), and share context (Cognition 2025, no data). See also [against](../../evidence/multi-agent-teams/against.md).
- **Check:** `task-decided` (exact): Decided holds a line "Why handed over:" with text after it; whether the reason is good is judgment.
- **Reader styles:** none.

## What it asks

- Put a line "Why handed over:" under Decided: what makes this work cheaper or better away from the lead.
- A reason is a property of the task: it can be checked by a program, it needs no context the lead holds, or it can run beside other work.
- With no such reason, do the work at the lead.

## Why

- Many agents do not reliably beat one: the gain is set by the task, from a large gain to a large loss (Kim 2025, measured).
- The predictive model in the same study explains a little over a third of the spread (R squared 0.373), so the reason is a judgment the lead writes down, not a rule it applies.
- A worker that lacks context makes implicit decisions the lead then finds out about (Cognition 2025, an owner's account, no data).

## Where it stops

- The line records the reason; it does not test it. No program decides that a task suits delegation.
- No source prices the time to brief and to check against the saving, so the reason cannot yet be a number.
- Kim 2025 measures systems of models on benchmarks, not a person handing work to a model.
