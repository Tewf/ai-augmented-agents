# 0006: The maestro protocol stays a convention, with no principle

**Decision:** The tier, order and cap rules are written as a protocol page and a program, graded convention, and none becomes a principle, because no run of this repository measures them.

**Status:** accepted, 2026-11-04. **Shape:** [decision record](https://github.com/Tewf/reader-ergonomics/blob/2e5b6f0c3e67d2dc8195a63cf882d46170e79a5a/shapes/decision-record.md).

## Context

- The map found that tiers are a cascade done by hand, batches are list scheduling with a barrier, and the cap is a greedy rule by cost alone ([the protocol](../protocols/maestro.md)).
- [Decision 0004](0004-which-findings-became-principles.md) makes a finding a principle when it is graded measured or supported with caveat and a program checks it.
- The pass share of a tier, the gain of a job and the cost of a job are not recorded for this repository's work. The only data is the maintainer's log of two days, read once.

## Decision

- The break-even rule and the critical path are arithmetic, so they are exact; that they save anything for one lead is untested.
- The comparison on the two days (7 waves needed, 12 steps taken) is one reading by the author of the file, with dependencies read from reasons in a log; it grades nothing.
- No principle is added. The protocol is graded convention.
- The exchange shape gains the Cost line (rule 8) and a check, because the quantities must exist before any rule here can be tested.

## Consequences

- **Good:** the claim is as strong as the evidence; the recorded costs are the way to earn a grade later.
- **Bad:** the rules are advice with a program, not a finding.
- When a month of Cost lines and verdicts exists, rank by estimated gain per cost against the cheapest-worker rule on the same jobs; a gain over it, or none, is the grade.
